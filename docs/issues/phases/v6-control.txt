E0001 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/motor_pid.py
Error     : Gain schedule jumps at the speed boundary
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 motor_pid.py
  gain: 0.9 -> 2.4 (speed 1.00 m/s)
  steering step visible in the log
WHAT HAPPENED
  The steering gain jumped from 0.9 to 2.4 the instant the robot crossed 1 m/s. The steering response suddenly felt twice as aggressive.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The gain schedule was a lookup table with discrete rows, and the lookup switched rows at the boundary instead of interpolating between them. A discontinuous gain produces a discontinuous control law: the controller's personality changes instantly at a speed threshold. Gain scheduling is only smooth when the schedule itself is smooth.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by linearly interpolating between table rows, and adding a test that verifies the gain never changes by more than 10% per control tick.

E0002 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/pid_calibrate.py
Error     : Feedforward sign is opposite on reverse
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 pid_calibrate.py
  reverse + feedforward = wrong direction
  robot pulls to the left in reverse
WHAT HAPPENED
  In reverse, the robot consistently pulled to the left. The feedback controller was compensating correctly - the feedforward term was actively fighting it.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The feedforward steering term was computed for forward motion and applied unchanged when reversing. Steering geometry is direction-dependent: the same curvature requires the opposite steer correction when the velocity is negative. The sign flip was known in the ackermann module but not propagated to the controller, so two modules disagreed about the direction convention.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by multiplying the feedforward term by the direction sign, and adding a reverse-path test case to the controller test suite.

E0003 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/motor_pid.py
Error     : Cubic spline overshoots past the goal
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 motor_pid.py
  end: x=2.01 y=0.03 (goal 2.00, 0.00)
  overshoot 1cm at the parking spot
WHAT HAPPENED
  The spline ended 1cm past the parking target. Small in absolute terms, fatal for a parking maneuver where the tolerance is measured in millimeters.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The smoothing pass relaxed the endpoint constraints, letting the spline drift off the fixed end point. Splines are defined by their boundary conditions; the endpoint condition was applied before smoothing and the smoother was allowed to move it. The constraint was lost in the pipeline, not in the math.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by re-imposing the fixed endpoint condition after smoothing and adding a unit test that pins both endpoints to the exact goal.

E0004 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/pid_calibrate.py
Error     : Velocity profile outputs negative speeds
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 pid_calibrate.py
  v: -0.2 m/s at t=1.2s
  robot reverses mid-run
WHAT HAPPENED
  The robot briefly reversed in the middle of a straight run. The velocity profile commanded a negative speed - the robot was doing exactly what it was told.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The trapezoidal profile computes the deceleration phase assuming the target is reachable in the given time. When the target was too close for the time budget, the deceleration term overshot below zero. The math produced an unreachable profile instead of detecting the conflict; negative speed is the profile's way of saying 'the plan is impossible'.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by clamping the profile speed to zero and extending the profile duration when the target is not reachable within the requested time.

E0005 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/motor_pid.py
Error     : Obstacle avoidance oscillates left-right
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 motor_pid.py
  steer: +12 -14 +11 -13 deg
  no progress toward the goal
WHAT HAPPENED
  The robot wobbled in front of an obstacle without making progress. The steering command swung positive and negative every half second.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The avoidance gain and the goal-pull gain fought each other: as the robot turned away from the obstacle it aligned with the goal, and as it turned toward the goal it approached the obstacle again. Two competing controllers with no arbitration produced a limit cycle. The oscillation is the system's equilibrium - it is stable in the wrong way.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by adding a deadband where neither controller acts and a goal bias that makes the goal-pull dominant whenever both demands conflict.

E0006 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/pid_calibrate.py
Error     : MPC solve fails on the first step
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 pid_calibrate.py
  cvxpy error: Problem status INFEASIBLE
  no control output
WHAT HAPPENED
  The model-predictive controller produced no output at all - the solver reported the optimization problem was infeasible at the very first step.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The MPC horizon covered more track than existed, and the terminal constraint required the robot to be at a goal point the path could not reach within the horizon. A constrained optimization with contradictory constraints is infeasible by definition; the solver correctly refused to solve an impossible problem. The model was the bug - the horizon and constraints did not match the track geometry.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by shrinking the horizon to the track length and relaxing the terminal constraint from an equality to a soft penalty.

E0007 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/motor_pid.py
Error     : PID output saturates then overcorrects
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 motor_pid.py
  output: 0.98 -> -0.99 -> 0.97
  robot snakes down the straight
WHAT HAPPENED
  The robot weaved down the straight at every launch. The PID output swung from full throttle to full brake and back, several times per second.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The integral term accumulated while the robot was stalled at launch, and by the time motion started the integral alone exceeded the output limits. The controller then had to burn off the stored error by overshooting in the opposite direction - classic integral windup. Windup happens because the integrator keeps summing even while the output is clamped at its physical limit, so the error it stores is never realizable.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by adding anti-windup: stop integrating when the output is saturated and the integral would push it further into the limit.

E0008 | v6.0 | SMALL | Found Day 101 | Fixed in 1 day | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/pid_calibrate.py
Error     : Stanley cross-track error is NaN at waypoint 0
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  $ python3 pid_calibrate.py
  cross_track: nan  (first waypoint)
  no steering command sent
WHAT HAPPENED
  The Stanley controller produced no steering command at the first waypoint. The cross-track error was NaN, so the controller output was NaN, and the car drove straight past the path.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The first path segment had zero length - two identical waypoints at the start of the list - so the tangent direction was undefined, and the cross-track math divided by zero. Stanley's formula assumes a valid segment direction; a zero-length segment violates that invariant. The NaN then propagated through the control output because no validation existed between the path math and the steering command.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by skipping zero-length segments in the path parser and replacing any NaN cross-track value with the previous valid value.

E0009 | v6.0 | BIG | Found Day 102 | Fixed in 2 days | Pi SSH terminal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/motor_pid.py
Error     : Global planner returns a path through the pillar
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  $ python3 motor_pid.py
  path waypoint 3: inside the pillar
  collision on the replay
WHAT HAPPENED
  The global planner produced a path that ran straight through a pillar. The replay showed the collision clearly - the planner never considered the robot's width.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The grid cost map marked only the obstacle's exact cells as blocked. A robot has physical width, so its center can be up to half a width away from an obstacle while the robot still collides. The planner used a point model for a 20cm-wide robot. Path planning for a real robot requires the configuration-space inflation; planning a point path through the environment is planning a point's path.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Replayed the planned path against the map - the waypoint was inside
  - Measured the clearance at the collision - less than the robot width
  - Inflated the obstacles by the robot radius and re-planned
FIX (took 2 days)
  Fixed by inflating all obstacles by the robot radius before running A*, and adding a clearance check on the final path.

E0010 | v6.0 | BIG | Found Day 102 | Fixed in 3 days | Pi SSH terminal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/pid_calibrate.py
Error     : Velocity profile ignores the corner limit
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  $ python3 pid_calibrate.py
  v = 2.0 m/s into a 60-degree corner
  tire squeal, robot drifts
WHAT HAPPENED
  The robot entered a 60-degree corner at full speed and slid wide. The profile optimized for time with no regard for what the tires can do.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The velocity profile minimized lap time subject only to acceleration limits, not to lateral acceleration. A corner has a speed limit set by physics: v_max = sqrt(mu * g * r). Ignoring the curvature limit produced a profile that is fast on paper and impossible in reality - the tires physically cannot provide the required lateral force.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Measured the actual cornering speed limit on the track
  - Overlaid the profile's speed at the corner - it exceeded the limit
  - Added a curvature-dependent speed cap to the profile
FIX (took 3 days)
  Fixed by adding a speed cap derived from the path curvature and the measured tire limit, applied at every point of the profile.

E0011 | v6.0 | BIG | Found Day 102 | Fixed in 4 days | Pi SSH terminal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/motor_pid.py
Error     : Cubic spline path is jerky at waypoint 5
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  $ python3 motor_pid.py
  acceleration spike: 12 m/s^2 at wp5
  visible jerk in the run
WHAT HAPPENED
  At waypoint 5 the robot jerked visibly. The path was smooth to the eye - the jerk was in the timing, not the shape.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The spline was parameterized by a non-arc-length parameter, so equal parameter steps cover unequal distances along the path. The velocity controller interpreted parameter speed as spatial speed, producing huge accelerations where the parameterization compressed distance. A geometrically smooth path can be dynamically jerky if the parameterization is uneven.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Plotted the path speed against distance - the spike matched wp5
  - Checked the parameterization - it was not arc length
  - Reparameterized by arc length and re-ran
FIX (took 4 days)
  Fixed by reparameterizing the spline with arc length so parameter speed equals spatial speed, and validating the acceleration profile in the profile_test.

E0012 | v6.0 | BIG | Found Day 102 | Fixed in 5 days | Pi SSH terminal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.0/pid_calibrate.py
Error     : Feedforward causes overshoot in sharp turns
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  $ python3 pid_calibrate.py
  ff: +0.8 -> overshoot 8 degrees
  corrective jerk after each corner
WHAT HAPPENED
  After every sharp corner the robot overshot the heading by 8 degrees and then corrected with a jerk. Gentle corners were fine - only the sharp ones overshot.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The feedforward gain was tuned for the average curve and over-applied on sharp ones. The steering response is nonlinear: at large angles the servo linkage has different leverage, so a feedforward command calibrated for 30 degrees is too strong at 90. A single constant gain cannot fit a nonlinear plant across the whole operating range.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Measured the overshoot against corner sharpness - it scaled with angle
  - Plotted the steer response vs command - nonlinear above 45 degrees
  - Scaled the feedforward with the corner curvature
FIX (took 5 days)
  Fixed by scaling the feedforward with the path curvature, and verifying with the corner_test that the overshoot stays under 2 degrees.

E0024 | v6.1 | BIG | Found Day 104 | Fixed in 5 days | Pi SSH terminal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v6.1/servo_step_test.py
Error     : Anti-windup test fails with 30% overshoot
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  $ python3 servo_step_test.py
  overshoot: 30% (target <10%)
  anti-windup not active
WHAT HAPPENED
  The windup test measured 30% overshoot - three times the target. The anti-windup code existed, was enabled, and did nothing.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The saturation check inside the anti-windup compared the output against the wrong limits - the controller's nominal range instead of the physical PWM limits. The controller output could never exceed its nominal range, so the saturation detector never triggered, so the anti-windup was permanently inactive. The feature was dead code with its documentation claiming otherwise.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Read the windup_test output - the saturation flag never fired
  - Compared the check limits with the real PWM limits - they differed
  - Fixed the limits and re-ran - overshoot dropped to 4%
FIX (took 5 days)
  Fixed by comparing against the physical PWM limits in the saturation check, and adding a test assertion that the anti-windup flag actually activates during a stall.
