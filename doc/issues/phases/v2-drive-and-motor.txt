E0001 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/drive_forward.py
Error     : Wheels spin the wrong way after boot
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ python3 drive_forward.py
  forward: left=+1.0 right=+1.0
  result: robot drives BACKWARD
WHAT HAPPENED
  The first drive test showed a robot that drove away from its operator while the log clearly said FORWARD. Hardware did what the pins said, not what the label said.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The motor channel macros were swapped relative to the physical wiring - channel A was driving the right motor and the code believed it was the left. PWM duty of +1 is just a number; the direction comes from the H-bridge enable pins, which were crossed. No software check can catch this; only a physical test with a known direction reveals it.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by swapping the two channel macros, then adding a boot self-test that spins each wheel by name and asks a human to confirm the direction.

E0002 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/motor_driver.c
Error     : Ackermann steer angle sign is flipped in a left turn
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ idf.py monitor
  left turn -> inner wheel angle +18deg
  expected inner wheel angle -18deg
WHAT HAPPENED
  The simulation produced perfect Ackermann angles, but the real robot turned the wrong way. The math was checked twice and was correct on paper.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The sign convention of the Ackermann geometry disagreed with the physical servo mounting. The robot's steering linkage pushes the wheel right when the servo angle is positive, so a mathematically positive inner-wheel angle turned the robot left. The model lived in the Pi, the mechanical convention lived in the chassis, and nothing connected the two until a physical turn test.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by negating the angle at the servo write boundary and adding a physical direction test to the turn_test script, with a comment documenting the mechanical convention.

E0003 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/uart_protocol.py
Error     : Encoder tick count stays 0
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ python3 uart_protocol.py
  ticks: 0, 0, 0, 0
  speed: 0.00 m/s (wheel never moved)
WHAT HAPPENED
  The wheel spun visibly, but the odometry reported zero ticks. The speed readout insisted the wheel was stationary while it was turning at full speed.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  During rewiring, the encoder's two signal wires were swapped with the motor's power wires. The encoder needs power and a clean signal; with power and signal crossed, the interrupt pin floated and never changed state. The encoder ISR never fired, so the tick counter stayed at zero - a silent failure because no error was ever raised, just wrong data.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by matching the connector pinout against the datasheet, then adding a sanity check that raises a warning when the wheel spins with zero ticks.

E0004 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/drive_forward.py
Error     : PID D-term spikes to NaN after a bump
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ python3 drive_forward.py
  error=0.002 dterm=nan
  pid output = nan
  motor ramps to full speed
WHAT HAPPENED
  The robot surged to full speed for a moment after rolling over a bump, then recovered. The D term was the only thing that looked strange.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The derivative was computed as (error - last_error) / dt with dt=0. A bump caused two identical encoder samples with a zero time delta, dividing by zero. Python returns NaN for 0/0, and NaN propagates through every subsequent arithmetic - the PID output became NaN, which compared false against all limits, so the safety clamp never triggered.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by adding a low-pass filter on the D term and replacing the division with a guarded version that keeps the previous value when dt is below a threshold.

E0005 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/motor_driver.c
Error     : Gyro reads 250 deg/s idle
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ idf.py monitor
  gyro: 248.3 deg/s  (expected ~0)
  imu status: SUSPECT
WHAT HAPPENED
  The robot sat perfectly still on the bench while the gyro claimed it was spinning at 248 degrees per second - a quarter of a full rotation per second, standing still.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The MPU6050 was running at its default full-scale range with factory offsets. Factory gyro offsets are rarely zero, and the raw value is scaled by the full-scale constant - with the 250 dps range selected the bias of the sensor element maps to tens of degrees per second. Calibration is not a refinement, it is a prerequisite: without the bias subtraction the reading is meaningless.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by writing the calibrated offset registers during init and re-running the v3.1 calibration whenever the board or mounting changes.

E0006 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/uart_protocol.py
Error     : Keyboard control ignores held keys
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ python3 uart_protocol.py
  key 'w' not detected during 'a' hold
  robot turns but never speeds up
WHAT HAPPENED
  Manual driving felt broken: holding 'w' while turning did nothing, even though the key worked alone. The robot turned, but never accelerated.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The keyboard handler used a blocking read, so while one key was held, the handler waited for that key to be released before reading the next. The terminal delivers one event at a time; a blocking getch() cannot represent two simultaneous keys. This is a fundamental limitation of line-buffered terminal input, not a bug in the key table.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by switching to raw mode with a non-blocking poll and maintaining a key-state table, which records every pressed key and merges them each control tick.

E0007 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/drive_forward.py
Error     : Duty value out of range 0-1023
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ idf.py monitor
  E (312) ledc: duty out of range!
  E (312) ledc: duty=4095, max=1023
  abort() was called at PC 0x42012345
WHAT HAPPENED
  The very first motor spin aborted the ESP32. The duty value was huge, and the LEDC driver refused it immediately.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The PWM timer was configured with 10-bit resolution (max 1023) but the speed calculation produced 14-bit values up to 4095. The mismatch came from two headers defining the resolution differently - pwm_config.h said 10 bits while the speed table was written for 14. The abort() is a hard assert inside the driver, by design: an out-of-range duty would produce undefined PWM behavior, so the firmware kills itself rather than corrupt the motors.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by clamping the duty in one place inside speed_control.c and asserting the resolution constant at boot, so both sides can never disagree again.

E0008 | v2.0 | SMALL | Found Day 21 | Fixed in 1 day | systemd journal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/motor_driver.c
Error     : Signed char overflow in speed command
Terminal  : systemd journal
  $ idf.py monitor
  I (401) uart: MOTOR left=200 right=-30
  E (402) motor: speed 200 overflows int8
  motor stuck at 127
WHAT HAPPENED
  The motor ran at half speed during calibration and ignored the requested value of 200. The command had clearly been received - it was printed in the log.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The UART parser stored speeds in an int8_t, whose maximum is 127. The value 200 overflowed to -56, which the driver then clamped to 127. The bug was a protocol/schema mismatch: the Pi generated a text protocol with no stated range, and the ESP32 guessed the width. Signed overflow in C is undefined behavior, so in principle anything could have happened - here it produced a silently wrong motor command.
FIX (took 1 day)
  Fixed by widening the parser to int16_t, adding a range check that rejects speeds outside -100..100, and documenting the protocol range in the header.

E0009 | v2.0 | BIG | Found Day 22 | Fixed in 2 days | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/drive_forward.py
Error     : Brownout detector was triggered
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  E (1200) esp_core_dump_flash: Core dump flash config is corrupted
  Brownout detector was triggered
  Rebooting... (loop)
WHAT HAPPENED
  The ESP32 rebooted exactly when both motors started. Every motor start killed the firmware in a loop.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The battery sagged below the ESP32's brownout threshold (about 2.8V) the moment both motors drew current. The brownout detector is a hardware safety circuit: when the supply dips, it holds the chip in reset. The reboot loop is the detector doing its job - the real failure was the power architecture: no bulk capacitance, thin power wires, and no low-battery cutoff.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Measured the supply voltage during motor start with a scope
  - Saw the sag to 2.6V exactly at the brownout event
  - Tested with a bench supply - no reboot, confirming the battery sag
FIX (took 2 days)
  Fixed by adding a 1000uF capacitor across the supply, thicker power wiring, and a low-voltage cutoff that refuses to start the motors below 3.2V.

E0010 | v2.0 | BIG | Found Day 22 | Fixed in 3 days | ESP32 serial monitor
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/motor_driver.c
Error     : rst:0x10 (RTCWDT_RTC_RESET), boot:0x13 (SPI_FAST_FLASH_BOOT)
Terminal  : ESP32 serial monitor
  rst:0x10 (RTCWDT_RTC_RESET), boot:0x13 (SPI_FAST_FLASH_BOOT)
  load:0x3fff0030,len:1184
  load:0x40078000,len:13260
  -> watchdog reset, boot loop
WHAT HAPPENED
  The ESP32 reset itself at random moments and rebooted. The reset reason code RTCWDT_RTC_RESET revealed the watchdog as the killer.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The motor task blocked on an I2C read that never completed, so it never yielded to the scheduler and never fed the task watchdog. After the configured timeout, the watchdog forced a reset - the system was not crashed, it was deadlocked, and the watchdog was the recovery mechanism. The I2C read had no timeout, which is what allowed the block to become infinite.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Decoded the reset reason from the boot banner
  - Confirmed the watchdog fired on the motor task specifically
  - Reproduced by inserting a blocked sensor read mid-run
FIX (took 3 days)
  Fixed by adding a timeout to the I2C read and feeding the watchdog in the motor task, so a blocked bus cannot kill the loop.

E0011 | v2.0 | BIG | Found Day 22 | Fixed in 4 days | Pi SSH terminal
----------------------------------------------------------------------
File      : history/v2.0/uart_protocol.py
Error     : serial.serialutil.SerialException: device reports readiness to read but returned no data
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  $ python3 final_drive.py
  serial.serialutil.SerialException: device reports readiness to read but returned no data
    File "pi/comm/uart.py", line 44, in read
WHAT HAPPENED
  The drive script crashed at startup on a strange serial error - the device said it had data, but the read returned nothing.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)
  The USB-UART adapter was half-seated, so the kernel's tty layer believed the device was present (it reported readability) while the adapter actually returned nothing. SerialException is raised when the read violates its own contract. Intermittent physical connections produce exactly this class of bizarre error: the software and the device disagree about the hardware state.
INVESTIGATION (before the fix)
  - Reinserted the adapter - the error vanished, confirming the hardware
  - Checked dmesg for USB disconnects during the run
  - Added a retry to see if the error was transient
FIX (took 4 days)
  Fixed by reseating the adapter and adding a retry-with-backoff around the read, with a clear 'check USB connection' message after three failures.

Error     : Odometry drift of 0.6m over a 2m run
Terminal  : Pi SSH terminal
  run distance: 2.0m  odometry: 2.6m

  The odometry claimed the robot traveled 2.6m when it actually traveled 2.0m. Every distance estimate was inflated by 30%.
WHY IT HAPPENED (root cause)

INVESTIGATION (before the fix)

  - Measured the true wheelbase with a caliper
  - Computed the correction factor from the measured distance
FIX (took 5 days)
  Fixed by measuring the true wheelbase and wheel diameter, updating odometry.py with the measured constants, and adding a 2m straight-line verification to the test suite.
